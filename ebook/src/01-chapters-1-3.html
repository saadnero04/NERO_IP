Chapter 01

Why one salary isn't enough anymore

If your money is gone before the end of the month, you are not bad with money. You have a math problem: one income has to cover more and more costs.

The real problem is the number of incomes, not the coffee

Most money advice is about cutting: skip the coffee, cancel subscriptions, cook at home. That helps, but cutting has a floor. You can't cut rent, transport or food below a certain level. Earning has no ceiling. A second income of even a few hundred per month changes how your month feels: you can save, pay down debt faster, or stop panicking when something breaks.

Why selling a service is the fastest second income

There are many ways to earn online: content, digital products, e-commerce, investing. Most of them take a long time before the first dollar arrives, because you first need an audience, stock or capital. A service is different: one client who pays you for one job is income this month. That's why this book starts with services. Later, once money comes in, you can add products and content.

Set your target

Vague goals like "make money online" don't work. Write down one number and one date. Make it realistic for a beginner, for example: "Earn my first 300 from clients within 60 days." Once you hit it, raise it.

Do this today

  1. Write your monthly costs on one line and your income on another. Note the gap.
  2. Decide how many hours a week you can give: 5, 10 or 15. Be honest.
  3. Write your first target: an amount, a date and the hours you'll put in.
Chapter 02

The 2026 opportunity: businesses need AI, not AI courses

Small business owners hear about AI every day. Most of them have no time to learn it. They will pay someone who just gets the result done.

What "selling AI services" means

You don't sell "AI". You sell a result a business already wants: more posts on Instagram, short videos for TikTok, faster replies to customers, better product photos, less admin work. AI tools let you produce that result much faster than before, so you can charge a fair price and still earn well per hour.

The business owner thinks:

"I know I should post every day and answer messages faster, but I'm running the shop. I don't have time."

You offer:

"I'll handle your 12 posts a month and 8 short videos. You approve them in 10 minutes a week."

Who your clients are

Start with businesses you can walk into or find on Google Maps. They are easy to reach and easy to understand:

  • Food: restaurants, cafés, bakeries, food trucks
  • Beauty and health: barbers, salons, nail studios, gyms, personal trainers, physiotherapists
  • Local services: plumbers, cleaners, driving schools, real-estate agents, car garages
  • Online sellers: small Shopify and Etsy shops, Instagram boutiques
  • Coaches and creators: people with knowledge but no time to make content

Why this works for a beginner

  • Low cost to start. Most tools have free plans or cost little per month.
  • Easy to prove. You can show a sample made for their business before they pay anything.
  • Repeat income. Social media, videos and chatbots are ongoing needs, so clients can pay you monthly.
  • The skill compounds. Every client makes you faster and gives you a new portfolio piece.

Be honest about the competition

Many people are trying this too. What wins clients is not "using AI". It's being reliable, specific to one type of business, and showing a real sample. The next chapters focus on exactly that.

Chapter 03

Pick your service: 5 options compared

Pick ONE service to start. Being "the person who does TikTok videos for restaurants" sells far better than "I do AI stuff".

ServiceWhat you deliverMain toolsDifficulty
1. Social media contentMonthly posts, captions, carousels, a content calendarChatGPT or Claude, CanvaEasy
2. Short-video editingReels/TikToks from the client's raw clips or AI visuals, with captions and musicCapCut, Descript, AI voice toolsEasy to medium
3. Customer-service chatbotA chatbot on their website or Instagram that answers FAQs and takes bookingsChatbot builders (e.g. Tidio, Voiceflow, Botpress, ManyChat)Medium
4. Product photos and adsClean product photos, lifestyle scenes and ad imagesAI image tools, background removers, CanvaEasy to medium
5. Admin automationAutomatic emails, invoice reminders, lead lists, spreadsheet workflowsZapier or Make, Google Sheets, AI assistantsMedium to hard

How to choose in 10 minutes

  1. What do you already like? Writing → option 1. Phone video → option 2. Tech and logic → options 3 or 5. Visual taste → option 4.
  2. What do local businesses around you obviously lack? Walk past 10 businesses or look them up on Instagram. Dead accounts? Bad photos? No website chat? That's your service.
  3. Can you show a sample within 48 hours? If yes, it's a good first choice.

The best beginner combo

Options 1 + 2 together ("posts + short videos") are the easiest to sell. Every business understands them and needs them every month. If you aren't sure, start there.

Pick a niche, too

Combine your service with one type of business: "Short videos for gyms", "Instagram content for restaurants", "Booking chatbots for barbers". A niche makes your samples, messages and prices simpler, and clients trust specialists more.

Do this today

  1. Choose one service and one niche. Write it as one sentence: "I help [niche] get [result] with [service]."
  2. Find 10 businesses in that niche near you or online, and save them in a spreadsheet.
