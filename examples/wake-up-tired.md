# Why You Wake Up Tired After 8 Hours (It's Not Your Sleep) 😴

**Problem:** Why do I wake up tired even after 8 hours of sleep?
**Hook:** "You slept 8 hours and still feel like a zombie? Your alarm might be the problem."

## Characters
- **Snooze** (problem) — look: a small round purple blob with heavy half-closed eyes, messy tuft of hair, oversized grey hoodie, holding a phone | voice: slow, sleepy, whiny
- **Dr. Zap** (fixer) — look: a tiny bright-yellow lightning-bolt character with round glasses and a white lab coat, energetic posture | voice: fast, upbeat, friendly

## Script & scenes
### Scene 1 (3s) — Snooze
> "I slept EIGHT hours. Why do I feel like a zombie?!"

_Visual:_ Snooze face-down on a desk at 7am, coffee cup knocked over, morning light through window, 3D Pixar style, vertical 9:16

### Scene 2 (6s) — Dr. Zap
> "Because your alarm woke you up in the middle of deep sleep. That groggy feeling is called sleep inertia."

_Visual:_ Dr. Zap pops in with a sparkle, points at a glowing sleep-cycle wave chart floating in the air

### Scene 3 (10s) — Dr. Zap
> "Sleep comes in about 90-minute cycles. Set your alarm to finish a cycle: 7 and a half hours, or 9 — not 8."

_Visual:_ the wave chart shows 5 waves, an alarm clock icon lands at the end of a wave and turns green

### Scene 4 (9s) — Dr. Zap
> "Then: same wake-up time every day, sunlight in the first 10 minutes, and no phone in bed."

_Visual:_ quick 3-panel montage: calendar with the same time circled, Snooze opening curtains to sunlight, phone flying into a drawer

### Scene 5 (6s) — Snooze
> "Wait… I've been waking up in the WRONG part of my sleep this whole time?"

_Visual:_ Snooze sits up wide-eyed, hair standing up, dramatic zoom

### Scene 6 (4s) — Dr. Zap
> "Yep. Try 7 and a half hours tonight and tell me how you feel."

_Visual:_ Dr. Zap winks at the camera, Snooze stretches energetically in the background

## Solution steps
1. Plan sleep in ~90-minute cycles (e.g. 7.5h or 9h) so you wake at the end of a cycle.
2. Wake up at the same time every day, weekends included.
3. Get sunlight within 10 minutes of waking.
4. Keep your phone out of bed. If tiredness continues for weeks, talk to a doctor.
