Chapter 04

Learn your skill in 7 days

You don't need a course. You need about an hour a day of focused practice on real examples. Here is the plan, day by day.

DayWhat to do (about 1 hour)
Day 1Create free accounts: an AI assistant (ChatGPT or Claude), Canva, CapCut. Study 5 businesses in your niche with great social media. Screenshot what you like.
Day 2Learn to prompt. Use the prompt formula below to write 10 captions and 3 content ideas for one imaginary business. Compare the results and keep the best prompt.
Day 3Learn the design or editing tool. Make 3 posts in Canva from templates, or edit 2 short videos in CapCut with auto-captions and music.
Day 4Copy a winner. Pick one great post or video from Day 1 and recreate its structure (not its content) for your imaginary business.
Day 5Speed run: produce a full "week of content" (5 posts or 3 videos) in under 2 hours. Write down where you lost time.
Day 6Make a system: save your best prompts, templates and a checklist so the next time is faster.
Day 7Review. Show your work to 2 people and ask: "Would a business pay for this? What looks amateur?" Fix the top 2 issues.

The prompt formula that works for any task

ROLE: You are a social media manager for [type of business] in [city]. GOAL: Write [number] [type of content] to get [result: more bookings / visits / orders]. AUDIENCE: [who buys: age, situation, what they care about]. STYLE: [friendly / premium / funny], short sentences, [language]. INCLUDE: [offer, opening hours, call to action, hashtags]. AVOID: [clichés, emojis overload, made-up prices or facts]. FORMAT: [numbered list / table with caption + image idea + hashtags].

Quality rule

Never send raw AI output to a client. Always edit it: fix the facts, add local details (street name, real menu items, real prices from the client), cut generic phrases. That editing is what you're paid for.

Video basics that matter most

  • The first 2 seconds decide everything. Open with movement, a face, food close-up, or a bold question on screen.
  • Always add captions. Many people watch with the sound off.
  • Keep it short. 15–35 seconds for most business videos.
  • Vertical 9:16, good light, steady shots. A phone is enough.
  • End with one action: "Book via the link", "Come try it this weekend".
Chapter 05

Build a portfolio with zero clients

Clients don't ask "how many years of experience do you have?". They ask "can you do this for me?". Answer with samples.

Three samples you can make this week

Sample 1: the "before / after" redo

Pick a real business with weak content. Without contacting them yet, make 3 better posts or 1 better video using their public photos, menu or products. Show it as a concept: "Concept I made for [Business]." Don't publish it as if they were your client.

Sample 2: the fictional brand

Invent a business in your niche (for example "Iron Den Gym") and produce one full month of content for it: 12 posts, 4 videos and a content calendar. This shows you can handle a whole package.

Sample 3: the free pilot (limit: 1–2)

Offer one or two businesses a free or very cheap one-week pilot, in exchange for a testimonial and permission to show the work. Stop after two. Free work is a tool, not a business model.

Where to show your portfolio

  • A one-page portfolio: a Canva website, Notion page or a simple PDF. Include your one-sentence offer, 6–9 samples, your packages and how to contact you.
  • Instagram or TikTok: a business account in your niche where you post your samples and tips. Clients often find you there.
  • Freelance profiles: Fiverr and Upwork let you add portfolio items (Chapter 6).

Portfolio checklist

  • Your one-sentence offer at the top
  • 6–9 samples, each with a one-line goal ("made to get more weekday bookings")
  • Concepts clearly labelled as concepts
  • 2–3 packages with starting prices
  • Contact: WhatsApp, email or booking link
Chapter 06

Find your first client

Run three channels at the same time: local outreach, online outreach and freelance platforms. Track everything in one spreadsheet.

Channel 1: local businesses (fastest for most beginners)

  1. List 30 businesses in your niche (Google Maps, Instagram, walking around).
  2. For each one, write one specific thing you could improve. Example: "last post 4 months ago".
  3. Make a quick free sample for your top 10 (1 post or a 10-second video).
  4. Send the message below, or walk in during a quiet hour and show it on your phone.
Hi [Name], I'm [Your name], I make short videos and posts for [niche] in [city]. I noticed [specific thing, e.g. your Instagram hasn't posted since June, but your reviews are amazing]. I made a quick sample for you, no strings attached: [link / attached]. If you like it, I can do [12 posts + 4 videos] every month so you don't have to think about it. Would you be open to a 10-minute chat this week?

Channel 2: online outreach (Instagram DMs and email)

Hey [Name]! Love what you do with [specific detail]. Quick idea: your [product/service] would do really well as short videos. I made one example for you here: [link]. Want me to make 3 more for free so you can test them?

Send 10–20 personal messages a day, not 200 copy-pasted ones. Personal messages get more replies and don't get your account flagged as spam.

Follow-up (send 3–4 days later if there's no reply)

Hi [Name], just bringing this back up in case it got buried. Happy to adjust the sample to your style. If now isn't the right time, no problem at all!

The numbers game

Most messages get no reply, and that's normal. Track your numbers weekly: messages sent → replies → calls → clients. If replies are low, improve your sample and first line. If calls don't convert, improve your offer and price.
